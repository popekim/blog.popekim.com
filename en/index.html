  • A Console Developer's Thoughts on the PS5 Jailbreak

    I've been developing console games for a long time. Compared to PC game development, console development has several advantages. Traditionally, I've considered two of them particularly important. First, the hardware specifications are fixed. On PC, developers have to account for all sorts of combinations of CPUs, GPUs, memory, and other hardware. On consoles, that isn't necessary. Since every user has essentially the same hardware, performance is much more predictable, and developers can optimize specifically for that hardware. For developers who enjoy squeezing every last bit of performance out of a machine, it's a pretty attractive environment. Second, security is strong. When you release a game on PC, piracy is something you have to worry about. On consoles, the platform manufacturer takes care of much of that. Instead of spending time implementing copy protection or fighting hackers, developers can concentrate on making better games. Of course, consoles have never been completely secure. Jailbreaks and piracy have existed for a long time. But they weren't particularly accessible to ordinary users, and some methods required hardware modifications. At least from a developer's perspective, consoles provided a much more comfortable environment than PCs. Then, recently, I came across some discussion about a PS5 jailbreak on Twitter. PS5 Relapse?   • A Developer's Guide to Living with Presbyopia

    If you want to keep working as a developer for a long time, taking care of your body is pretty important. Sure, it's mostly brain work, but you still need a body that can keep up. So I've always made an effort to stay reasonably healthy. That doesn't mean hitting the gym and working out like crazy every day. For me, it's mostly about eating well, sleeping well, and avoiding unnecessary strain on my body. When something does start hurting, though, I get a little more proactive. Years ago, when I started having shoulder pain, I switched to a tenkeyless keyboard. Without the number pad, I could keep my mouse closer to my body. Eventually, I got rid of the conventional mouse altogether and switched to a roller mouse. When my back started giving me trouble, I spent some time doing intensive strength training. I also got a standing desk and eventually invested in a Herman Miller chair. That's generally how I've dealt with these things. When something goes wrong, I try to figure out what's causing it and change something, whether it's exercise, equipment, or daily habits. But no matter how well you take care of yourself, some things are unavoidable as you get older. One of them is presbyopia, the age-related loss of the ability to focus on nearby objects.
